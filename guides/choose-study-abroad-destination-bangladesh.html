How to choose a study abroad country from Bangladesh

By the Independent Education counselling team · Updated 6 November 2026

There is no single "best" country. The right one is where your results, budget, subject and long-term plans fit together. This guide gives you the questions to ask, then a short comparison of the destinations Bangladeshi students choose most.

Please check before deciding: visa rules, fees and work rights change often, sometimes several times a year. We link each point to the official government page. If a page says something different from this guide, the official page is right.

Start with five questions

  1. What can you afford, every year? Add tuition, living costs, visa and health fees, flights and the money you must show for the visa. Some countries ask you to show a fixed amount in your bank for a set period.
  2. What do you want to study? Some subjects (for example nursing, medicine, law, teaching) have country-specific licensing rules. A degree from one country may not let you work in that profession in another.
  3. What are your academic results and English score? These decide which universities and scholarships are realistic.
  4. Do you want to stay and work after graduating, or come home? Post-study work options differ a lot between countries.
  5. When can you start? Most countries have one or two main intakes. Applications often close 6–10 months before.

Budget: build your own number

We do not quote one fixed cost per country here, because it depends on the city, the university and the exchange rate on the day you pay. Ask each university for its current tuition, and use the country's official visa page for the amount you must show for living costs. A counsellor can help you put these into one yearly figure in Bangladeshi Taka.

Scholarships worth looking at

  • Chevening (UK, master's, fully funded; check the current year's eligibility and deadline)
  • Australia Awards (check whether Bangladesh is listed for the current round)
  • DAAD (Germany)
  • Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters (Europe)
  • University scholarships: many universities give automatic or merit-based discounts. Ask each one.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing a country only because a friend went there.
  • Paying a deposit before checking that the course fits your visa and career plan.
  • Showing bank funds that were added suddenly, without a clear source. Visa officers may ask where the money came from.
  • Applying too late for the intake you want.
