How to write a statement of purpose that stands out

By the Independent Education counselling team · Updated 18 November 2026

Your statement of purpose (SOP) is the one part of your application where the university hears you in your own words. A good one is specific, honest and clearly about this course.

Write it yourself. Universities and visa officers can recognise copied or ghost-written statements, and some use software to check. A counsellor can guide and give feedback, but the words should be yours.

A simple structure

  1. Opening: what draws you to this subject, shown through a real moment or experience, not a quotation.
  2. Your background: your studies, projects, results and any work experience that prepared you.
  3. Why this course: specific modules, research areas, facilities or teaching that match your goals.
  4. Why this university and country: concrete reasons, not general praise.
  5. Your goals: what you want to do after the course, and how it connects to Bangladesh or your career plan.
  6. Closing: a short summary of why you are a good fit.

What admissions teams look for

  • A clear reason for choosing this subject
  • Evidence of preparation: results, projects, reading, work
  • Realistic goals that the course supports
  • Good written English and a clear structure

Common mistakes

  • One generic statement sent to every university
  • Repeating your CV line by line instead of explaining it
  • Exaggerated claims you cannot support
  • Ignoring the word limit or the questions the university asks
  • Unexplained gaps in study; explain them briefly and honestly

Before you submit

  • It answers every question the university asks
  • It is within the word or character limit
  • The university's and course's names are correct
  • You have read it aloud and checked spelling and grammar
  • Someone you trust has read it and given feedback
