Your study abroad timeline: a 12-month plan

By the Independent Education counselling team · Updated 18 November 2026

Most problems we see come from starting too late. Use this plan for a September start and move everything earlier or later for other intakes.

Every university and country is different. Some deadlines, especially for scholarships and competitive courses, are earlier than shown here. Check each one.
WhenWhat to do
12 months before (September)Decide your goals and budget. Book a free counselling session. Start researching countries and courses.
10–11 months beforeShortlist universities. Note every deadline. Start preparing for IELTS, PTE or TOEFL. Check scholarship dates.
8–9 months beforeTake your English test. Collect and attest certificates and transcripts. Ask referees for letters. Draft your statement of purpose.
6–8 months beforeSubmit university applications. Apply for scholarships that are open.
4–6 months beforeReceive offers and compare them. Accept one and pay the deposit if required.
3–4 months beforeGet your CAS, CoE, I-20 or acceptance letter. Prepare proof of funds. Book TB tests, medicals or biometrics if needed.
2–3 months beforeApply for your student visa. Arrange accommodation and health insurance.
1 month beforeBook flights. Open a way to send money. Attend a pre-departure briefing. Pack your documents.
ArrivalCollect your residence permit if needed, attend orientation and register with your university.

For January or February intakes

Start the same plan about 12 months before your course: tests by around April, applications by June to August, and your visa from October.

Key tips

  • Some visas, such as the UK Student visa, can only be applied for up to 6 months before the course starts.
  • Visa appointment waits vary. For Germany, the embassy in Dhaka reports waits of more than 27 months for self-funded students, so register very early.
  • Keep copies of everything in one folder.
