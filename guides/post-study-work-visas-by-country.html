Post-study work visas by country: what changes in 2026 and 2027

By the Independent Education counselling team · Updated 18 November 2026

The chance to work after graduating is a big reason students choose a country. The rules differ a lot, and several changed in 2026 or are changing in 2027.

This is general information, not immigration advice. Eligibility depends on your course, its length, where you studied and when you apply. Always check the official page before you choose a course for its work rights.
CountryVisa or permitUsual lengthKey points
United KingdomGraduate visa2 years now (3 for PhD); 18 months for bachelor's and master's graduates who apply from 1 January 2027PhD graduates keep 3 years. If your degree is awarded in 2026, apply before the end of 2026 to keep 2 years.
CanadaPost-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP)Up to 3 years, depending on your programmeSome college programmes must be in an eligible field of study.
AustraliaTemporary Graduate visa (subclass 485)Depends on your qualificationCheck eligibility and current rules with the Department of Home Affairs.
New ZealandPost-study work visaUp to 3 years after a bachelor's or higherEligibility was extended from 118 November 2026, and a 6-month Short-Term Graduate Work Visa opened. You can usually get only one.
IrelandStamp 1G12 months after a Level 8 degree; up to 24 months after Level 9 or higherLets you look for work or work full time.
GermanyJob-seeker residence permit18 monthsYou can work in any job while you look for qualified work.
USAOptional Practical Training (OPT)Usually up to 12 months, with a possible 24-month extension for eligible STEM degreesRules have been under discussion; check the current position with your university and USCIS.
South KoreaD-10 job-seeker visaVariesAllows a job search, with some internships and part-time work.
ChinaWork permit—Master's and PhD graduates of Chinese universities can apply within a year of graduating without the usual two years' work experience.
MalaysiaGraduate PassNot available to Bangladeshi graduatesYou would need an employer-sponsored Employment Pass.

Questions to ask before you choose a course

  • Is the course long enough to qualify for post-study work?
  • Is the field of study eligible (important for some Canadian college programmes)?
  • Do you need to apply within a set time after your results?
  • Can family members join or work?
