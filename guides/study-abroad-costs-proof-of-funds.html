Study abroad costs and proof of funds by country

By the Independent Education counselling team · Updated 18 November 2026

Your budget has two parts: what you will actually spend, and the money you must show for your visa. They are not the same number, and the second is set by each government.

Check before you transfer money. These amounts were checked in November 2026 and change often. Some come from official pages and some only from reliable secondary sources; we say which. Always confirm the current amount on the official page before you apply.

What to include in your budget

  • Tuition fees for the first year (and how much the university wants as a deposit)
  • Living costs: rent, food, transport, phone and study materials
  • Visa fee, and health cover or the health surcharge where it applies
  • Flights, plus money for your first weeks before you settle
  • Test fees, document attestation and courier costs at home

How to show your funds well

  • Keep the money in the account for the full period the rules require, without large unexplained deposits.
  • If a parent sponsors you, prepare their income proof, tax documents and a document that shows your relationship.
  • An education loan sanction letter can count as evidence in many countries; check the rules for yours.
  • Make sure names and amounts match across every document.

Ways to reduce costs

  • Apply for scholarships early: see our scholarship guide.
  • Consider cities with lower rent, or shared accommodation.
  • Compare one-year master's courses (common in the UK and Ireland) with two-year courses.
  • Check which countries let students work part-time, and how many hours.
