Scholarships for international students

Scholarships can cover part or all of your tuition and sometimes living costs. Most are merit-based, rewarding strong academic results, while some are need-based. Check what your university and destination offer before you rely on family funds or an education loan.

Scholarships for international students are offered by governments, universities and private organisations. Some are fully funded and pay for almost everything, while others give a fixed amount or a percentage off your tuition fees. Fully funded government scholarships are highly competitive and their deadlines are early, so most students combine one or two of them with university scholarships, which are easier to win.

Use the search below to find scholarships by destination and study level, then read on to learn how scholarships work, what they cover and how to make your application stand out.

Check my scholarship options
Study level

Eligibility, funding and deadlines change every year, and some scholarships are only open to certain countries. Always check the official scholarship website before applying. Independent Education does not award these scholarships, but we can help you prepare your application.

Types of scholarships

Government scholarships

Funded by governments, often fully funded and highly competitive, such as Chevening and GKS.

University scholarships

Offered by universities, usually as tuition discounts for strong students.

Merit-based awards

Based on academic results, leadership and achievements.

Need-based aid

Based on your family's financial situation, mostly in the USA.

Subject or country awards

For specific fields of study, regions or groups of students.

What a scholarship can cover

Every scholarship is different. A fully funded scholarship may include all of these, while a partial scholarship usually covers only part of your tuition fees.

Tuition fees

All or part of your course fees, paid to the university or taken off your fee invoice.

Living allowance

A monthly payment towards rent, food and travel, mainly with fully funded government scholarships.

Flights

Return flights between Bangladesh and your study country, with some government scholarships.

Health insurance

Medical cover during your studies, included with some fully funded schemes.

Language or preparation year

Some schemes, such as GKS and the Chinese Government Scholarship, can include a language course before your degree.

How to apply for a scholarship

  1. Check your eligibility

    Read the rules for your nationality, study level, subject, academic results and English score. Some scholarships are only open to students from certain countries, including Bangladesh.

  2. Choose your course and university

    Many scholarships need an offer or an application to a university first, and university scholarships are often considered automatically when you apply.

  3. Prepare your documents

    Collect certificates, transcripts, your English test result, a CV and your passport. Some scholarships need documents attested in Bangladesh, which can take several weeks.

  4. Write your essays

    Answer the scholarship's own questions. Show your academic strengths, leadership, community work and how the course fits your career plans.

  5. Arrange recommendation letters

    Ask teachers or employers who know your work well, and give them your CV, your goals and plenty of time.

  6. Apply before the deadline

    Submit everything on the official website, keep copies, and prepare for an interview if the scholarship has one.

Documents you'll usually need

  • Academic certificates and transcripts (SSC, HSC or degree)
  • English test result (IELTS, PTE or TOEFL)
  • Statement of purpose or scholarship essays
  • Recommendation letters
  • CV or résumé
  • Passport
  • University offer letter, where required
  • Proof of work experience, where required

Tips to improve your chances

  • Start 12 to 18 months before your intended intake
  • Read the eligibility rules carefully before you apply
  • Write essays specific to each scholarship, never one copy for all
  • Show leadership, community work and clear career goals
  • Ask referees early and give them your CV and goals
  • Apply to several scholarships, not just one
  • Never pay anyone who promises a guaranteed scholarship

Scholarship timeline

  1. 12–18 months before

    Research scholarships and courses, and take your English test

  2. 9–12 months before

    Prepare essays and references, and apply to universities

  3. 6–9 months before

    Submit scholarship applications and attend interviews

  4. 3–6 months before

    Receive results, accept your offer and apply for your visa

Scholarship questions

Can Bangladeshi students get a fully funded scholarship?

Yes. Several government scholarships are open to Bangladeshi students, including Chevening and Commonwealth Scholarships in the UK, the Global Korea Scholarship (GKS), the Chinese Government Scholarship, Türkiye Scholarships, Erasmus Mundus in Europe and Fulbright in the USA. They are very competitive, so apply for university scholarships as well.

Do I need IELTS to get a scholarship?

Most scholarships ask for an English test result, and many set a minimum IELTS or TOEFL score. Some accept other tests or a medium-of-instruction letter. Check the rules for each scholarship.

Can I get a scholarship for a bachelor's degree?

Yes, although there are fewer than for master's degrees. GKS undergraduate, the Chinese Government Scholarship, Türkiye Scholarships and many university scholarships are open to bachelor's students.

When should I start applying?

Start 12 to 18 months before your course begins. Many fully funded scholarships close 8 to 12 months before the start date.

Do I have to pay to apply?

Official scholarships never charge a fee to guarantee a place. Be careful of anyone who asks you to pay for a guaranteed scholarship. University application fees may still apply.

Can Independent Education apply for a scholarship for me?

We don't award scholarships, and you must apply yourself on the official website. Our counsellors help you find scholarships you may be eligible for, plan your timeline and prepare strong essays and documents.

For a calendar of when the main scholarships usually open, read our guide: Scholarships for Bangladeshi students: what to apply for, and when.

Scholarship and intake updates

Recent changes to scholarships, intakes and student visa rules in our main study destinations. Last checked on 18 November 2026; the Netherlands, Bulgaria, Finland, Sweden, Denmark, Switzerland, Poland, Hungary, Malta, Cyprus, Lithuania and Spain were added on 26 November 2026. Rules change often, so always confirm the latest details with the official source before you apply.

Australia
  • The 2027 National Planning Level stays at 295,000 new international students, the same as 2026. It is a planning level, not a hard cap: applications above a university's allocation are still accepted but processed more slowly (Study Australia).
  • Students moving from an Australian school, or from an affiliated pathway provider or TAFE, into a public university are outside the planning level.
  • The Student visa (subclass 500) fee rose to A$2,500 on 1 July 2026 (Fragomen). The Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485) is reported at A$5,750.
  • The living-cost amount you must show for the visa is reported as about A$29,710 a year for one student. Confirm it on the Department of Home Affairs website.
  • Australia Awards: the 2027 round closed on 30 April 2026. Dates for the next round have not been announced; past rounds opened around February.
  • Main intakes: February/March and July. Some universities offer more.
United Kingdom
  • The Graduate Route falls from 2 years to 18 months for bachelor's and master's graduates who apply on or after 1 January 2027. PhD graduates keep 3 years. If your degree is awarded in 2026, apply before the end of 2026 to keep the longer period.
  • The government plans an international student levy of £925 per international student, charged to universities from the academic year starting 1 August 2028. Draft legislation was published in July 2026 (GOV.UK).
  • Chevening 2027/28: applications closed on 6 October 2026. Interviews run March to April 2027 and results come from mid-June 2027. The next round has usually opened in August.
  • Main intakes: September, with a smaller January intake.
Canada
  • From 1 September 2026, a single applicant outside Quebec must show C$23,448 for living costs, up from C$22,895. Tuition and travel are extra (CIC News).
  • Since 1 January 2026, master's and doctoral students at public institutions no longer need a Provincial or Territorial Attestation Letter (PAL/TAL). Most other students still do.
  • The 2026 target is 408,000 study permits, including extensions. The 2027 cap and provincial allocations had not been confirmed when we checked, so check IRCC.
  • Main intakes: January, May and September.
USA
  • On 16 November 2026, a US federal court cancelled (vacated) the rule that would have replaced "duration of status" with fixed stays of up to 4 years for F-1 and J-1 students. The existing rules stay in place unless the decision is changed on appeal (NAFSA).
  • Reports differ on whether the December 2025 travel proclamation restricts student visas for Bangladeshi applicants. Check with the US Embassy in Dhaka before you apply.
  • Fulbright 2027–28 for Bangladesh closed on 11 July 2026. The next round has usually opened around the middle of the year.
  • Main intakes: fall (August/September) and spring (January).
New Zealand
  • From 16 November 2026, Immigration New Zealand extended post-study work visa eligibility, and a new six-month Short-Term Graduate Work Visa opened. Check INZ for who qualifies.
  • Eligible students can work up to 25 hours a week during study.
  • Manaaki New Zealand Scholarships: published dates for the next round differ between sources. Check the official Manaaki website for dates and the list of eligible countries.
  • Main intakes: February and July.
Ireland
  • Government of Ireland International Education Scholarship: 60 awards a year for master's and PhD study, each with a €10,000 stipend and a tuition fee waiver. The 2026 call closed on 12 March 2026; the next call has usually opened in spring.
  • You can work 20 hours a week during term and 40 hours during holidays, and you must show €10,000 for living costs.
  • After graduating, Stamp 1G lets you stay 12 months after a Level 8 degree, or up to 24 months after Level 9 or higher.
  • Main intakes: September and January.
Europe
  • Erasmus Mundus: 37 new joint master's programmes were selected for the September 2027 intake. Application dates differ by programme, mostly between October 2026 and January 2027. Apply directly to each programme, in the scholarship round.
  • DAAD scholarships in Germany: deadlines differ by programme, so check each one on the DAAD website.
  • Germany: the winter semester starts around October and the summer semester around April.
Netherlands
  • NL Scholarship (formerly Holland Scholarship) 2027–28: €5,000 in your first year for non-EEA students starting a full-time bachelor's or master's at a participating Dutch university. It is not a full-tuition scholarship. The application period is open; each university sets its own deadline, often 1 February or 1 May 2027 (Study in NL).
  • Main intake: September. Some programmes also start in February.
Bulgaria
  • International students pay tuition fees at state universities, and these are higher than for home students (Eurydice). We did not find an open government scholarship for Bangladeshi students, so ask each university about fee discounts.
  • Main intake: the academic year usually starts in September or October.
Finland
  • Joint application for studies starting in August/September 2027: 7–21 January 2027 at Studyinfo.fi. You can choose up to six programmes, and some universities open separate applications from late 2026 (Study in Finland).
  • Many universities offer scholarships for gifted non-EU students who pay tuition fees. You are usually considered after admission, so check each university.
  • Main intake: August/September. A smaller number of English-taught programmes start in January.
Sweden
  • Swedish Institute Scholarships for Global Professionals, autumn 2027: Bangladesh is on the list of eligible countries. The scholarship covers full tuition, SEK 12,000 a month and a SEK 15,000 travel grant. You need work and leadership experience (Swedish Institute).
  • First apply to the master's programmes at University Admissions by 15 January 2027. Then apply for the SI scholarship from 8 to 24 February 2027. Results are expected on 22 April 2027.
  • Main intake: August/September, with a smaller January intake.
Denmark
  • Danish Government Scholarships are for non-EU/EEA students. There are only a few on each master's programme. Depending on the university they give a full or partial tuition waiver, and some add a monthly grant. At many universities, your programme application also counts as your scholarship application (Aarhus University).
  • DTU offers tuition fee waivers for excellent non-EU/EEA master's applicants for the autumn intake only. Apply by 15 January (DTU).
  • Main intake: September. Some programmes also start in February.
Switzerland
  • Swiss Government Excellence Scholarships 2027–28: mainly for researchers who already hold a master's degree, with CHF 2,450 a month. You need a Swiss academic supervisor. Applications opened on 20 August 2026 and go through the Swiss embassy (SERI). Check with the Swiss Embassy in Dhaka whether Bangladesh is included this year, and what its deadline is.
  • Main intakes: September (autumn semester) and February (spring semester).
Poland
  • NAWA government programmes are open only to certain countries. Bangladesh was not on the Łukasiewicz programme list when we checked (NAWA). Ask universities about their own fee discounts.
  • Main intake: October. Some programmes also start in February.
Hungary
  • Stipendium Hungaricum 2027–28: Bangladesh is a partner country, through the Ministry of Education, for bachelor's, master's, one-tier master's (including medicine) and PhD study in any field (Stipendium Hungaricum). The scholarship covers full tuition and a monthly stipend, plus a free dorm place or a housing allowance.
  • The deadline has usually been in mid-January (expected around 15 January 2027, not yet confirmed). You also need a nomination from the Ministry of Education in Bangladesh.
  • Main intake: September. Some programmes also start in February.
Malta
  • University of Malta: 40% to 100% tuition fee waivers for non-EU/EEA students accepted onto a Master by Research or a PhD. The waiver depends on your interview score, and it does not cover living costs. The last deadline was 30 April 2026 for October starts (University of Malta).
  • Main intake: October. Some programmes also start in February.
Cyprus
  • The Cyprus State Scholarship Foundation gives awards to students from outside the EU too: up to €3,000 a year for a bachelor's and €4,000 a year for a master's (Study in Cyprus). Many universities have their own scholarships, so ask each one.
  • Main intakes: September/October and January/February.
Lithuania
  • Lithuanian state scholarships for master's study are only for listed countries, and Bangladesh was not on the 2026 list. The 2026 call is closed (Study in Lithuania).
  • Vilnius University offers five full tuition waivers for non-EU/EEA master's applicants. Apply by 1 April 2027 (Study in Lithuania).
  • Main intake: September. Some programmes also start in February.
Spain
  • MAEC-AECID 2026–27 scholarships were mainly for Spanish nationals, Latin America and Africa. The only Asian option was a diplomacy master's for public-sector officials. That call closed on 3 June 2026 (UAM).
  • Most Bangladeshi students rely on university scholarships and fee discounts, and on Erasmus Mundus programmes (see Europe above).
  • Main intake: September/October. Some programmes also start in February.
South Korea
  • Global Korea Scholarship for undergraduates (GKS-U) 2027: the embassy track opened in September 2026. Check the closing date with the Korean Embassy in Dhaka or Study in Korea.
  • The graduate scholarship (GKS-G) 2027 had not been announced when we checked. Past calls have opened around February.
  • Main intakes: March and September.
Malaysia
  • In November 2026, Malaysia's education services agency (EMGS) said more than 1,000 scholarship places had been arranged through partnerships, and that admission checks for international students are being tightened.
  • Malaysia International Scholarship (MIS) 2026/27 closed in April 2026. The next round has not been announced.
