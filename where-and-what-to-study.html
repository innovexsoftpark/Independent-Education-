Where and what to study?

We're here to help you explore your study options without stress, from picking a country to choosing a course. It's wonderful that you're thinking about studying abroad. Have you decided on a university or a course yet?

Studying abroad is more than getting a degree from a foreign university. It's a life-changing journey. You may learn a new language, you live in a different culture and you adjust to a new way of learning. All of this can help your job prospects, and it also changes how you see the world.

With so many universities and programmes around the world, it's hard to know where to start. How do you pick the right place and the right course?

Base your decision on what matters to you. Ask yourself:

  • Which country do you dream of living in?
  • What kind of programme interests you?
  • Which universities match your goals and your personality?
  • How much is the tuition, and is the academic quality good?
  • Would you rather study in a lively city or somewhere quiet, with peace and space?

You don't need to rush. Take your time, think through your choices and compare the pros and cons. If you still feel unsure, talk to our counsellors. We're here to help, and our advice is free.

  • USA

    Why students choose it

    Famous universities, a strong focus on research and innovation, flexible degree choices and a range of scholarships. Student visas limit off-campus work.
    Read more about studying in the USA

    The United States is famous for universities such as Harvard, MIT and Stanford, which offer a wide choice of programmes and advanced research facilities. The US also has a strong culture of new ideas and starting businesses, which helps students build skills that employers around the world value.

    Tuition can be high, but many universities offer scholarships and other funding. Depending on the university you choose, you could live in a big city or a close-knit college town, so you can enjoy city energy or a quieter setting while you earn your qualification. More than one million international students study in the US, according to US government SEVIS data.

    International students must follow US visa rules, which affect how much you can work and how long you can spend outside the country. F-1 students may work on campus for up to 20 hours a week during term and full-time during official breaks, such as summer. Off-campus work is only allowed with permission from your university or from US Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS). Students who spend more than five months in a row outside the US, away from their study programme, can lose their student status. These rules change often, so check the latest official information or ask a counsellor.

    Well-known universities (examples)
    Harvard, MIT, Stanford, University of California campuses
    Popular fields
    Engineering, computer science, business, sciences

  • United Kingdom

    Why students choose it

    Historic universities, many one-year master's degrees, rich cultural life and a post-study route for graduates to look for work.
    Read more about studying in the UK

    The United Kingdom is known for its long history and high-quality education. Universities such as Oxford and Cambridge are often ranked among the best in the world, and universities across England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland offer many shorter courses, such as one-year master's degrees, as well as scholarships for international students.

    Studying in the UK means a strong education in a friendly, multicultural setting. You can explore historic sites, join festivals such as Bonfire Night and watch Shakespeare's plays, or enjoy modern city life in London, Manchester, Edinburgh, Cardiff, Glasgow and Belfast.

    While studying for a degree, most students may work up to 20 hours a week during term and full-time during official holidays. After graduating, you can apply for the Graduate Route visa to stay and look for work. It currently lasts two years (three for PhD graduates). For bachelor's and master's graduates who apply on or after 1 January 2027, it will be 18 months; PhD graduates keep three years. These rules change often, so check the latest official information or ask a counsellor.

    Well-known universities (examples)
    Oxford, Cambridge, Imperial College London, University of Edinburgh
    Popular fields
    Business, law, engineering, medicine and health

  • Australia

    Why students choose it

    High-quality education, a multicultural lifestyle, part-time work rights and beautiful natural surroundings. The government has recently tightened international student numbers.
    Read more about studying in Australia

    Australia is popular with international students for its high-quality education, internationally ranked universities and wide choice of courses. It offers a multicultural lifestyle, good options for work after study and a high standard of living, so many students feel at home while they enjoy a diverse community.

    Part-time work rights add to the appeal. Most students may work up to 48 hours per fortnight during study periods and unlimited hours during scheduled course breaks. Master's by research and PhD students can work unlimited hours once their course has started.

    Universities have dedicated support services that help with accommodation, visa questions and settling in, and local multicultural communities help students feel welcome. Australia also has amazing natural places to explore, from the Great Barrier Reef and the Outback to Whitehaven Beach and the Gondwana Rainforests.

    The government now manages how many new international students can start each year. The planning level for 2026 is 295,000 new students, and visa applications are prioritised for institutions that stay within their allocation, so places at popular universities can be more competitive. You normally need a Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) from your university before you apply for your student visa.

    After graduating, you may be able to apply for a Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485) to stay and work. Under the current rules, it lasts up to two years after a bachelor's or coursework master's degree and up to three years after a research master's or PhD, with age limits. These rules change often, so check the latest official information or ask a counsellor.

    Well-known universities (examples)
    University of Melbourne, University of Sydney, ANU, UNSW
    Popular fields
    Business, IT, engineering, nursing and health

  • Canada

    Why students choose it

    Co-op programmes with paid work experience, post-graduation work permits and welcoming multicultural cities. Study permit limits have been introduced recently.
    Read more about studying in Canada

    Canada attracts students from all over the world with its high-quality education and wide range of programmes. Many courses include co-op placements, where you learn through real work. Co-op helps you gain practical skills and experience, build contacts with possible employers and improve your chances of finding a job after you graduate.

    International students can usually work off campus up to 24 hours a week during term, up from the old limit of 20 hours, and full-time during scheduled breaks. After graduating, you may be eligible for a Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP). College and other programmes below bachelor's level must be in an eligible field of study; bachelor's, master's and PhD graduates do not have this requirement. Canada work experience can also help if you later apply for permanent residence.

    Students enjoy cultural diversity, a high quality of life and a welcoming environment with a lively mix of traditions, celebrations and food. Multicultural cities such as Toronto, Vancouver and Montreal help students from around the world feel at home.

    It is important to plan ahead. Canada limits the number of study permits it issues. For 2026, it plans to issue up to 408,000 study permits, including 155,000 for new students. Most students also need a letter from the province or territory where they will study. These rules change often, so check the latest official information or ask a counsellor.

    Well-known universities (examples)
    University of Toronto, University of British Columbia, McGill University
    Popular fields
    Engineering, computer science, business, health

  • New Zealand

    Why students choose it

    Research-focused universities, a relaxed lifestyle, post-study work options and stunning scenery.
    Read more about studying in New Zealand

    New Zealand is a popular place to study abroad, with good universities, stunning nature and a relaxed way of life. Its welcoming people and safe, peaceful and inclusive environment help international students feel at home.

    New Zealand has internationally ranked universities that offer a wide range of programmes, with a strong focus on research and innovation. Students can also apply for scholarships, work part-time while they study and apply for a post-study work visa after graduating. Living costs depend on the city and your lifestyle, so it is worth comparing them with other destinations.

    Most tertiary students on a new student visa can work up to 25 hours a week during the semester, up from 20 hours since 3 November 2025. After graduating, the Post Study Work Visa can let you stay and work for up to three years, depending on your qualification. Under recent changes, students who complete at least 30 weeks of a Postgraduate Diploma and then start a master's degree may qualify for a one-year visa based on the diploma alone. Work experience in New Zealand can also help if you later apply for residence. These rules change often, so check the latest official information or ask a counsellor.

    Beyond your studies, New Zealand's landscapes offer plenty of adventure, from Aoraki/Mount Cook and Mount Ruapehu to Piha Beach, Cathedral Cove, Fiordland National Park and Abel Tasman National Park. It is a great place to balance study, work and an active lifestyle.

    Well-known universities (examples)
    University of Auckland, University of Otago, Victoria University of Wellington
    Popular fields
    IT, agriculture, engineering, business

  • Ireland

    Why students choose it

    Respected universities, an English-speaking EU country and a strong tech and pharma job market. Proof-of-funds requirements for students have recently changed.
    Read more about studying in Ireland

    Ireland has lively cities, friendly people and a rich culture, making it a welcoming place for international students. It is home to universities such as Trinity College Dublin, University College Dublin and University College Cork, known for high academic standards and research, with programmes across many subjects.

    Irish culture is rich in music, dance and literature, and its landscapes, from the Cliffs of Moher to the Burren, add to the charm. People are known for their hospitality and traditions, and because English is the main language, it is easy to communicate and settle in. Tuition can be lower than in some other English-speaking destinations, and many international companies have offices in Ireland, especially in technology, pharmaceuticals and finance.

    Students with Stamp 2 permission can work up to 20 hours a week during term, and up to 40 hours a week from 1 June to 30 September and from 15 December to 15 January. After graduating, the Third Level Graduate Programme (Stamp 1G) lets you stay to look for work: up to 12 months after an honours bachelor's degree and up to 24 months after a master's or PhD.

    Plan your finances early. Since 30 June 2025, students on courses longer than eight months, both those who need a visa and those who do not, must show access to at least €10,000 for the first year, on top of tuition fees. Students who need a visa, including students from Bangladesh, must show the same amount for each later year.

    These rules change often, so check the latest official information or ask a counsellor.

    Well-known universities (examples)
    Trinity College Dublin, University College Dublin, University of Galway
    Popular fields
    Data analytics, computer science, pharma, business

  • Europe

    Why students choose it

    Many public universities charge low or no tuition (for example in Germany and Norway), there are thousands of English-taught master's programmes, and a student permit lets you travel across the Schengen area. Rules and costs differ by country.
    Read more about studying in Europe

    Europe offers many countries within easy reach of each other and thousands of English-taught programmes, especially at master's level. In some countries, such as Germany, most public universities charge little or no tuition, while other countries charge international students fees, so costs vary a lot.

    A student residence permit from a Schengen country usually also lets you visit other Schengen countries for short stays of up to 90 days in any 180-day period.

    Work and post-study rules depend on the country. In Germany, for example, international students may work 140 full days or 280 half days a year, or 20 hours a week during lectures, and graduates can get a residence permit for up to 18 months to look for a job. Learning the local language helps a lot with jobs and daily life.

    These rules change often, so check the latest official information or ask a counsellor.

    Well-known universities (examples)
    Technical University of Munich, KU Leuven, University of Amsterdam, Politecnico di Milano
    Popular fields
    Engineering, computer science, business, design

  • South Korea

    Why students choose it

    Modern, technology-focused universities, the government-funded Global Korea Scholarship and a safe, fast-paced city life. Part-time work is allowed with permission from immigration.
    Read more about studying in South Korea

    South Korea has modern, technology-focused universities, safe and fast-paced cities and a rich culture. The government-funded Global Korea Scholarship supports international students at bachelor's, master's and PhD level.

    Students on a D-2 visa need a part-time work permit before they start working. Weekly limits depend on your level of study and your Korean language level (TOPIK): for example, bachelor's students who meet the Korean requirement can work up to 25 hours a week during semester, but only 10 hours without it. Master's and PhD students can work more. Learning Korean makes a big difference to work, daily life and jobs after graduation.

    These rules change often, so check the latest official information or ask a counsellor.

    Well-known universities (examples)
    Seoul National University, KAIST, Yonsei University, Korea University
    Popular fields
    Engineering, IT, business, Korean studies

  • Malaysia

    Why students choose it

    Lower tuition and living costs, branch campuses of UK and Australian universities and a short flight from Bangladesh.
    Read more about studying in Malaysia

    Malaysia is a short flight from Dhaka, with tuition and living costs that can be lower than in many Western countries. It hosts branch campuses of UK and Australian universities, such as Monash and Nottingham, as well as well-known Malaysian universities. English is widely used in universities and daily life, and the country has a multicultural society.

    Student pass applications for universities go through Education Malaysia Global Services (EMGS). International students may work part-time only during semester breaks or holidays of more than seven days, for up to 20 hours a week, in approved jobs and with Immigration Department approval arranged through their institution.

    These rules change often, so check the latest official information or ask a counsellor.

    Well-known universities (examples)
    Universiti Malaya, Monash University Malaysia, University of Nottingham Malaysia, Taylor's University
    Popular fields
    Business, IT, engineering, hospitality

  • Türkiye

    Why students choose it

    Tuition and living costs are often lower than in Western Europe, many programmes are taught in English, and the government offers Türkiye Scholarships. Students can work with a work permit.
    Read more about studying in Türkiye

    Türkiye connects Europe and Asia and has a long history, a rich culture and modern cities such as Istanbul and Ankara. Tuition and living costs are often lower than in Western Europe, many programmes are taught in English, and the government offers Türkiye Scholarships for international students.

    A student residence permit does not give you the right to work. Your employer must apply for a work permit for you. Bachelor's students can usually only work after their first year, part-time, while master's and PhD students can apply from their first year.

    These rules change often, so check the latest official information or ask a counsellor.

    Well-known universities (examples)
    Boğaziçi University, Middle East Technical University (METU), Koç University, Özyeğin University
    Popular fields
    Medicine and health sciences, engineering, business, architecture

  • China

    Why students choose it

    Strong universities in engineering, technology and medicine, close to Bangladesh, with government and university scholarships such as the Chinese Government Scholarship (CSC). Part-time work and internships need university and immigration approval.
    Read more about studying in China

    China has strong universities in engineering, technology and medicine, is close to Bangladesh and offers scholarships such as the Chinese Government Scholarship (CSC) as well as university scholarships. Many programmes are taught in English, though learning Chinese helps a lot with daily life.

    International students may only work or do internships off campus with approval from their department and university, a letter from the host company and an internship note added to their residence permit by the local exit-entry office. Without it, off-campus work is not allowed.

    If you plan to study medicine (MBBS) and work as a doctor in Bangladesh, check that the university and programme are recognised by the Bangladesh Medical and Dental Council (BMDC) before you apply.

    These rules change often, so check the latest official information or ask a counsellor.

    Well-known universities (examples)
    Tsinghua University, Peking University, Fudan University, Zhejiang University
    Popular fields
    Engineering, computer science, medicine (MBBS), business, Chinese language

Universities listed are well-known examples only, not partner institutions or rankings. Visa and work rules change often, so check the latest official information or ask a counsellor.

Frequently asked questions

How do I choose the right course?

Start with the subjects you enjoy and the career you want, then check entry requirements, course content, accreditation and graduate outcomes. A counsellor can help you compare options.

How do I choose the right country?

Think about your budget, your subject, work rights during and after study, visa rules, climate and lifestyle. Our destination guide compares the main options.

How much does it cost to live abroad?

It depends on the country and city. Big cities usually cost more. Plan for rent, food, transport, phone, insurance and study materials, and ask us for a current estimate.

Can I get a scholarship?

Many universities and governments offer scholarships for international students, usually based on academic merit. Deadlines are often months before the intake, so start early.

Not sure where or what to study?

Tell us your subject, budget and goals, and we'll shortlist countries, courses and universities that suit you, free of charge.
